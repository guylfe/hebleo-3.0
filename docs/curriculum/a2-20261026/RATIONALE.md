Each letter lesson has two intervening lessons. Those lessons actively reuse known letters. The point is to spend the new reading access on useful language, not merely to finish the alphabet sooner. Letter completeness is only one gate: sound patterns, meanings, forms and likely replies also need teaching.

## Why common imperatives fit

Yes: בוא, שב and תן produce useful actions immediately, support picture/action checks, and have a small, teachable set of addressee forms. I use בוא/בואי/בואו, שב/שבי/שבו and תן/תני/תנו at 18. The come/sit meanings then anchor infinitives at 21. The more general family analysis comes later. Source בא remains the same meaning; the command surface is taught explicitly, without claiming it is a new lexeme.

Context is part of the teaching: invitations and passing things between familiar people are suitable starting situations. Bare תן לי is not presented as a universally polite service request. בבקשה is added when ק arrives. No rule of לא + affirmative imperative is taught; prohibition remains later.

Bolozky's research documents common short imperatives and register distinctions: [Colloquial Hebrew imperatives revisited](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.langsci.2008.12.008), Language Sciences 31 (2009), 136–143. This supports selecting usable spoken forms; it does not establish an optimal course order.

## Exercise rationale

Use meaning → retrieval → a changed situation. Each practical outcome needs a reply the learner can understand. For example, request water, hear that only milk is available, and accept or decline it. The next attempt changes the available drink. Prompt independent speech before revealing text, and let optional practice vary the cue. This checks comprehension and adaptation, not only visible-tile arrangement.

Letter lessons remain honest reading/sound outcomes. Group sessions can later reveal whether the learner can perform these tasks; spreadsheet checks do not establish spoken competence.

## Reconsider: costs and unresolved choices

1. **כ/פ/ג load.** One lesson contains five consonant sound values and two final forms. The draft uses separate pages and cumulative checks. Splitting it would add a letter lesson and, with two intervening topics, move alphabet completion later unless another gap changes.
2. **Six-word minimum.** It is retained. Some early vocabulary sets and later returns are still substantial; nine targets plus a new construction may be too much even when every word is useful. A small number of justified exceptions may be preferable to finding extra words merely to reach six. No such exception was silently applied.
3. **Opportunity cost.** Roots move 29→33, four-form present work 24→30, plurals 20→25 and regular Pa’al 36→41. Singular wants and common actions are earlier, but comprehensive formal preparation is later. Detailed ו/י spelling moves 10→36; early spelling is modelled word by word, with the systematic return made explicit.
4. **Not every move is earlier.** The larger question set moves 18→22, but מי/מה and yes/no questions are used much earlier. This buys space for availability/wants/commands. Full numbers move 35→28; counting still does not dominate the opening.
5. **Video cost.** The new letter groupings and rewritten intermediate teaching require a source-video cut/re-recording review. No A2 video reuse or audio production was verified in this task. Existing A first20 app drafts have not been replaced.
6. **Later broad-color spacing.** First 53 lessons have zero topic-family gap violations. The inherited later flags are 61→63, 87→89, 100→102, 102→103, 115→117; each involves integration/reuse sharing a broad sentence-grammar color. They remain visible for joint review. They are not silently equated with teaching identical new grammar twice.
