This led to a case study I did with a student named Adam. He came to me with Zero Hebrew knowledge, but similar scientific background. Screen 4: Give the number meaning · MEMORY-A2.learn.connection
LEARNER TEXT:
That was quite a lot of digits to remember! Now let’s look at the same number in a different way:
07/04/1776 · 09/11/2001
These dates use American month–day–year order: July 4, 1776, and September 11, 2001.
The first is the date of the American Declaration of Independence; the second is the date of the September 11 attacks. If you already know those events, the digits now connect to something familiar.
We haven’t changed the number. We’ve changed what it means to you: instead of sixteen unrelated digits, you can think of two dates you already know. If those dates are unfamiliar, the connection will be less helpful. The useful starting point is something you already know.

FEEDBACK after “The digits were rearranged into a different number.”: The order stayed the same. We recognised a meaning within it.

Check — understanding
MEMORY-A2.check.useful-anchor — multiple choice
LEARNER PROMPT: When you use this idea to learn something new, what should you look for?
OPTIONS / TOKEN POOL:
A. A connection to something you already know.
B. The most complicated story you can invent.
C. A way to avoid ever practising it again.
ANSWER / ACCEPTED MODEL: A connection to something you already know.
FEEDBACK after “A connection to something you already know.”: Yes. Start with what’s familiar and use it to make sense of the new information.
FEEDBACK after “The most complicated story you can invent.”: Your connection can be simple. What matters is whether it helps you bring the new information back to mind.
FEEDBACK after “A way to avoid ever practising it again.”: A connection gives you a useful starting point. We’ll also practise retrieving what you learn and return to it later in the course.

Apply — complete exercise set
MEMORY-A2.apply.personal-connection — self-check
LEARNER PROMPT: Think of a date you already know. How would knowing its meaning help you remember the digits? You can keep your example private and explain it to yourself.
ANSWER / ACCEPTED MODEL: Any familiar date with an explanation linking its meaning to its digits. Example: “New Year’s Day is January 1, so I can recover 01/01 by thinking of the start of the year.” This is a self-check with no single required date or wording.
FEEDBACK: Ask yourself: did I already know the event, and can thinking of it bring the date back to mind? The same idea can help with a new word, a name or something else you want to learn.
