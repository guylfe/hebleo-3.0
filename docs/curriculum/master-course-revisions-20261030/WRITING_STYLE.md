## Preserve

- A personal teacher speaking to the learner: “I”, “you”, “we” and natural invitations such as “Let’s…”. Guy’s own experience belongs in the introduction; new biographical claims or success stories must not be invented.
- Connected explanation: establish why an activity matters, let the learner experience it, explain what happened, and connect the result to the next lesson or everyday learning.
- Guy’s established teaching examples and demonstrations. Inspect the human source before inventing an alternative. For MEMORY-A2, the source is the original presentation’s ten-second string `0704177609112001` and its two date associations, not the generated cup/key/apple activity.
- Short, natural paragraphs with purposeful page breaks. Learner prose addresses the learner; timer rules, answer keys, IDs and production notes remain separate author specifications.
- Concrete explanations and encouraging transitions. Feedback refers to what the learner just tried; it does not assume that everyone improved or remembered perfectly.
- Questions serve a specific teaching purpose. Guy's later 30 October comments explicitly reject repetition to fill a question quota; the lesson determines how many questions it needs.
- A demonstration may deliberately expose difficulty before an explanation. It does not automatically become a mastery exercise or a repeat-until-correct task. MEMORY-A2 uses a first attempt and one comparison, with no compulsory retry.
